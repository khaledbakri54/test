Frequently Asked Questions

A Vehicle Power of Attorney (POA) is a legal document that authorizes another person (the attorney-in-fact) to act on your behalf regarding vehicle-related matters such as selling, registering, or transferring the title of your vehicle.

Common situations include: you are selling a vehicle but cannot be present, you need someone to handle DMV paperwork on your behalf, you are deployed military and need vehicle matters handled, or you are purchasing a vehicle through an agent.

Requirements vary by state. Many states require notarization for a vehicle POA to be valid. We recommend having your document notarized to ensure it is accepted by your local DMV or relevant authority.

The validity period depends on the expiration date you set in the document. If no date is specified, it typically remains valid until revoked by the principal. Some states may have maximum validity periods.

Yes, a principal can revoke a POA at any time by providing written notice to the attorney-in-fact. It is recommended to also notify any institutions (DMV, insurance companies) that may have a copy of the original POA.
